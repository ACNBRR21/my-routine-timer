1. The Harvard Timeboxing Method

According to extensive research synthesized by the Harvard Business Review ("How Timeboxing Works and Why It Will Make You More Productive") , timeboxing consistently ranks as the #1 most effective productivity technique.

The method consists of three sequential steps executed every morning:

  1. Step 1 — Brain Dump: Empty everything in your mind onto paper or screen. No filtering, no sequencing.
  2. Step 2 — Top 3 Priorities: Select the absolute 3 items that must move forward today, giving them prime mental slots.
  3. Step 3 — Timebox: Assign a definitive start time and end time to every task. Open-ended "to-do lists" invite Parkinson's Law; timeboxing imposes deliberate scarcity.

2. Two Kinds of Items: Anchors vs. Flow

A fatal flaw in standard timeboxing is treating all blocks identically. In real business life, commitments belong to two distinct classes:

  • Anchors = Calendar Meetings. They are hard wall-clock commitments involving other people. Anchors are immovable: they are never shifted, never delayed, and never split. Anchors always win.
  • Flow = Personal Execution Tasks. Deep work blocks, writing, triaging, and habits. Flow tasks are elastic: they slide forward, pull forward, or split into parts to weave around Anchors.

3. Real-Time Deterministic Reflow

Plans break the moment real work begins. The Anchor & Flow engine is current-time aware and self-healing. Every few seconds, the engine compares your actual progress against the computer clock:

A. Task Overruns & The Drift Chip

If a flow task takes longer than planned and the next anchor is safe, subsequent flow tasks slide forward automatically. A small, non-intrusive drift chip appears (e.g. +7 min) showing your day's schedule adjustment.

B. Automatic Mid-Day Task Splitting

If a running flow task overruns and threatens an immovable meeting, the engine automatically splits it:

Automatic Split Rule:
• Strategic Deck — Part 1 ends at the meeting's exact Scheduled Start.
• Client Status Review (Outlook) executes untouched.
• Strategic Deck — continued resumes the minute the meeting finishes.

C. Early Finish Pull-Forward

If you finish a task early, tapping Stop Task immediately pulls all subsequent flow tasks forward. Your afternoon ends earlier, eliminating procrastination in artificial gaps.

4. Exporting Your Daily Meetings (.ICS)

The "Plan My Day" chat accepts .ics calendar files to instantly import your anchors.

Outlook Calendar (Web & Desktop):

  1. In Outlook Web, click Settings (gear) → Calendar → Shared Calendars, choose your calendar under Publish a calendar, and click ICS → Download.
  2. In Outlook Desktop, open Calendar, go to File → Save Calendar, choose Today, and save as an .ics file.

Google Calendar:

  1. Click Settings (gear) → Settings → Import & Export → Export to download a zip file containing your calendars.
  2. Extract the .ics file for your primary calendar and drag it into the Plan My Day chat.
Timezone Assumption (v1):
All ICS event timestamps are assumed to match your browser's current local timezone. Timed single events are supported; recurring and all-day events are ignored in v1.

5. Minimal Control: Two Choices on Pause

In adherence to Steve Jobs' minimalist philosophy, there are no confusing panels or dialogue boxes. When paused, you have only two choices:

  • (a) Slide Over: Need more time? Extend the current task; the engine slides downstream flow forward.
  • (b) Stop Task: Done ahead of time? Finish now; the engine pulls downstream flow forward immediately.
