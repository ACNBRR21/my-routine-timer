1. The Problem with Conventional Timers

Traditional productivity tools enforce one of two broken models:

  • Rigid Calendar Blocks: You schedule your day down to 15-minute slots. A single phone call or delayed meeting pushes every task off schedule, inducing guilt and anxiety.
  • Abstract Stopwatches (Floating Timers): A simple Pomodoro timer measures duration (e.g., 25 mins) in a vacuum. It has no awareness that you have an immovable client call at 2:00 PM, leading to collisions.

The Solution: "Anchor & Flow" divides your workday into two entities:

  1. Hard Anchors: Non-negotiable wall-clock commitments (client syncs, webinars, standups).
  2. Fluid Flow: Flexible tasks, deep work sessions, and health habits (squats, hydration) that dynamically flow through the open time windows between your anchors.

2. Generating Schedules via AI Prompting

You can paste your raw Outlook or Google Calendar day view directly alongside your daily to-do list into an LLM (such as Gemini, Claude, or ChatGPT) using the master prompt.

How to Export / Copy from Outlook:

  1. In Outlook (or Outlook Web), switch to Day View or open your Agenda.
  2. Copy the text of your day's meetings (e.g., 10:00 AM - 10:45 AM: Product Sprint, 2:30 PM - 3:00 PM: 1:1 with Anil).
  3. Open your LLM, paste the provided PROMPT.md, and append your meeting text and unorganized tasks.

The Required 5-Column Schema:

Routine,Task Name,Duration (Mins),Parent Task,Scheduled Start
Work Day,Morning Inbox Triage,20,Morning Alignment,
Work Day,Hydrate & 10 Squats,2,Morning Alignment,
Work Day,Sprint Standup (Outlook),30,,10:00
Work Day,Deep Work: API Architecture,50,,
Work Day,Executive Sync (Outlook),45,,14:30

Notice how Scheduled Start is populated with 24-hour wall-clock times (e.g., 10:00, 14:30) for calendar meetings, but left completely blank for flexible tasks. The timer automatically treats non-empty start times as Hard Anchors.

3. The Execution Engine in Practice

  • Calendar Anchor Radar: The application continuously monitors your computer's clock. When an anchor approaches, the radar bar displays: 📅 Next Anchor: Executive Sync at 14:30 (in 12m).
  • Overtime Count-Up: When a task reaches 00:00, the timer does not abruptly reset. It flashes red, sounds a gentle harmonic alert, and counts upwards (+00:01, +00:02) to measure actual task overrun.
  • Mid-Task Time Extension: If you realize a task needs more time—even while the alarm is ringing—tap +5m, +10m, or +30m. The alarm silences immediately, and the countdown resumes with the extended budget.
  • Finish Early (⏩): Completed a 30-minute block in 18 minutes? Tap Finish Early ⏩. The timer records the exact elapsed wall-clock seconds and immediately fires up the next step.
  • Zen Focus Mode (⊙): Tap the Zen button or press Z to strip away all peripheral checklists, tables, and settings, leaving only the task at hand and the timer clock.

4. Auditing Your Estimation Velocity (The Secret to Productivity)

Most productivity systems fail because they do not track the Planning Fallacy—the cognitive bias where humans consistently underestimate the time required to complete work.

At the end of your day, click Export CSV. You receive an audit file formatted as:

Routine,Task Name,Type,Scheduled Anchor,Actual Start Time,Actual End Time,Target Duration,Time Added,Actual Elapsed,Variance,Status
Work Day,Deep Work,Fluid Flow,,11:04:12,11:58:30,3000,600,3258,+258,Overtime
Work Day,Client Review,Calendar Meeting,14:30,14:30:05,14:58:12,1800,0,1687,-113,Finished Early
The Key Calibration Metric: The Estimation Multiplier
$$\text{Estimation Multiplier} = \frac{\text{Actual Elapsed (Secs)}}{\text{Initial Target Duration (Secs)}}$$

How to Use This in Excel or Google Sheets:

  1. Calculate Your Personal Task Multiplier: In your spreadsheet, add a column: =Actual_Elapsed / Target_Duration.
    • If your multiplier is 1.35, you routinely take 35% longer than you think. In future schedules, automatically multiply your initial estimates by 1.35.
  2. Analyze Category Variance: Group by Parent Task or Routine using a Pivot Table. You will often find that technical writing has a 1.4x multiplier while administrative tasks sit at 0.9x.
  3. Identify Transition Friction: Compare the Actual End Time of Task $N$ with the Actual Start Time of Task $N+1$. Gaps greater than 5 minutes reveal hidden leakage (context switching, tab-surfing, or distraction).
